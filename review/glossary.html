Glossary: added and revised definitions for review

49 definitions were written for the site and 9 booklet definitions were revised; the other 210 are from the Year 12 Economics Booklet. Edit glossary/data/glossary.json, then run the Glossary build and build/build_hover.py.

Added

Allocative efficiency
T4 · 2026-11-13		Resources are used to produce the goods and services that consumers value most.
Bracket creep
T4 · 2026-11-13		When inflation or wage growth pushes income earners into higher tax brackets, raising their average tax rate without a change in tax law. Also called fiscal drag.
Circular flow of income
T3 · 2026-11-13		A model of the economy showing the flows of income and spending between households, firms, the financial sector, government and the overseas sector.
Commodity prices
T2 · 2026-11-13		The world prices of raw materials such as iron ore, coal, gas and wheat. They strongly influence Australia's terms of trade and the value of the dollar.
Competition and Consumer Act 2010
T4 · 2026-11-13		The Commonwealth law, enforced by the ACCC, that prohibits anti-competitive conduct and protects consumers. It replaced the Trade Practices Act 1974 in 2011.
Counter-cyclical policy
T4 · 2026-11-13		Policy that works against the business cycle: stimulating demand in a downturn and restraining it in a boom.
Crawling peg
T2 · 2026-11-13		A flexible peg that is adjusted frequently in small steps. Australia used a crawling peg against a trade-weighted basket of currencies from 1976 to 1983.
Currency crisis
T2 · 2026-11-13		A sudden, sharp fall in a currency's value, often when a central bank runs out of the reserves it needs to defend a fixed or pegged exchange rate.
Decentralised wage determination
T4 · 2026-11-13		A system where wages and conditions are set by bargaining at the level of the enterprise or the individual, not by a central tribunal for whole industries.
Deflation
T3 · 2026-11-13		A sustained fall in the general level of prices.
Disinflation
T3 · 2026-11-13		A fall in the rate of inflation: prices are still rising, but more slowly.
Dynamic efficiency
T4 · 2026-11-13		The ability of firms and the economy to adapt to change and innovate over time.
Economic development
T1 · 2026-11-13		A broad measure of welfare that covers not only income per person but also health, education, the distribution of income and the quality of the environment.
Emissions trading scheme
T4 · 2026-11-13		A market-based policy that caps total emissions and lets firms buy and sell permits to emit, putting a price on carbon.
Exchange rate
T2 · 2026-11-13		The price of one currency in terms of another, such as the number of US dollars needed to buy one Australian dollar.
External stability
T2 · 2026-11-13		An economy's ability to meet its financial obligations to the rest of the world in the long run, judged by the current account, foreign liabilities and the exchange rate.
Flexible peg
T2 · 2026-11-13		An exchange rate system in which the authorities peg the currency at a set rate and defend it, but adjust the peg when it moves too far from the market rate. Australia used flexible pegs from 1971 until the float in December 1983.
Free trade agreement
T1 · 2026-11-13		An agreement between two or more countries to reduce or remove barriers to trade between them. Also called a preferential trade agreement.
Full employment
T3 · 2026-11-13		The level of unemployment at which there is no cyclical unemployment and inflation is stable. See NAIRU.
Inflation
T3 · 2026-11-13		A sustained increase in the general level of prices, measured in Australia by the Consumer Price Index.
International division of labour
T1 · 2026-11-13		The way the stages of producing goods and services are spread across countries, with each stage located where it can be done most efficiently.
Macroeconomic policy
T4 · 2026-11-13		Policies that influence the overall level of demand in the economy: fiscal policy and monetary policy.
Modern awards
T4 · 2026-11-13		Industry or occupation-based minimum pay and conditions set by the Fair Work Commission. They form the safety net with the National Employment Standards.
National minimum wage
T4 · 2026-11-13		The lowest hourly rate that can legally be paid to an adult employee not covered by an award or agreement, set each year by the Fair Work Commission.
Negative externality
T3 · 2026-11-13		A cost of production or consumption that falls on third parties and is not reflected in the market price, such as pollution.
Net primary income
T2 · 2026-11-13		Income earned by Australian residents from overseas (interest, dividends and wages) minus income paid to non-residents. It is usually the largest deficit item on the current account.
Net zero emissions
T4 · 2026-11-13		A balance between the greenhouse gases emitted and those removed from the atmosphere. Australia has a legislated target of net zero by 2050.
OECD
T1 · 2026-11-13		The Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development: a forum of mostly advanced economies that researches and promotes policies for economic growth and living standards.
Output gap
T3 · 2026-11-13		The difference between actual output and the economy's potential output. A negative gap means spare capacity; a positive gap adds to inflation.
Paris Agreement
T4 · 2026-11-13		The 2015 international treaty in which countries set their own emissions targets to limit global warming to well below 2°C. It succeeded the Kyoto Protocol.
Political constraints
T4 · 2026-11-13		Limits on economic policy that come from the need to win public and parliamentary support, which can delay or weaken reforms.
Protection
T1 · 2026-11-13		Government action that gives domestic producers an advantage over foreign competitors, such as tariffs, subsidies, quotas, local content rules and export incentives.
Quantitative easing
T4 · 2026-11-13		A central bank buying government bonds and other assets to lower long-term interest rates when the cash rate is already near zero.
Real wages
T3 · 2026-11-13		Wages adjusted for inflation, showing the change in what wages can actually buy.
Recession
T3 · 2026-11-13		A period of falling economic activity, commonly defined as two consecutive quarters of negative growth in real GDP.
Regional business cycle
T1 · 2026-11-13		The fluctuations in economic activity shared by the economies of a region, such as East Asia, because of their close trade and financial links.
Reserve assets
T2 · 2026-11-13		Foreign currency and gold held by the central bank. They can be sold to buy the domestic currency when the central bank intervenes in the foreign exchange market. Changes in them are recorded in the financial account.
Safeguard Mechanism
T4 · 2026-11-13		The Australian Government scheme that sets declining emissions limits (baselines) for the country's largest industrial facilities.
Social wage
T3 · 2026-11-13		Government-provided benefits in cash or in kind, such as Medicare, public education and welfare payments, that add to household living standards.
Speculation
T1 · 2026-11-13		Buying or selling a financial asset, such as a currency, in the hope of profiting from a change in its price.
Stagflation
T3 · 2026-11-13		Rising inflation at the same time as weak growth and rising unemployment, usually after a negative supply shock.
Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)
T1 · 2026-11-13		The 17 goals adopted by United Nations members in 2015 to end poverty, protect the environment and improve living standards by 2030.
Technical efficiency
T4 · 2026-11-13		Producing the maximum output from a given quantity of inputs, or a given output at the lowest cost. Also called productive efficiency.
Trade liberalisation
T1 · 2026-11-13		The removal or reduction of barriers to trade, such as tariffs and quotas, so that goods and services move more freely between countries.
Transfer payments
T3 · 2026-11-13		Payments from government to individuals for which no good or service is received in return, such as the Age Pension and JobSeeker.
Trimmed mean inflation
T3 · 2026-11-13		The RBA's preferred measure of underlying inflation. It removes the 15% of items with the largest price rises and the 15% with the smallest.
United Nations (UN)
T1 · 2026-11-13		The global organisation of 193 member states that promotes peace, development and co-operation, including through the Sustainable Development Goals.
Valuation effect
T2 · 2026-11-13		The change in the Australian-dollar value of foreign debt and assets caused by a movement in the exchange rate, with no new borrowing or lending.
Wage Price Index (WPI)
T3 · 2026-11-13		The ABS measure of the change in hourly rates of pay, used as the main indicator of wages growth.

Revised from the booklet

Aggregate supply
T3 · 2026-11-13		The total quantity of goods and services that firms in an economy are willing and able to produce at each price level. Aggregate supply increases with improvements in productivity, technology and the quantity of resources. On an AD–AS diagram this is the upward-sloping curve, labelled AS. It is also called short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) when the diagram shows the vertical long-run aggregate supply curve (LRAS) as well.
Australian Competition and Consumer Commission
T4 · 2026-11-13		An independent Commonwealth statutory authority that enforces the Competition and Consumer Act 2010 (formerly the Trade Practices Act 1974). The ACCC promotes competition and fair trading, assesses mergers, protects consumers and regulates national infrastructure services.
Fair Work Australia
T3 · 2026-11-13		The name of the national workplace relations tribunal from 2009 until 1 January 2013, when it was renamed the Fair Work Commission. See Fair Work Commission.
G7/8
T1 · 2026-11-13		The Group of Seven (G7) is a forum of the leaders of seven major advanced economies: Canada, France, Germany, Italy, Japan, the United Kingdom and the United States. The European Union also takes part. It began in 1975 as a group of six and became the G7 when Canada joined in 1976. Russia joined in 1997, making it the Group of Eight (G8), but was suspended in 2014 after its annexation of Crimea, so the group is the G7 again. The leaders meet at an annual summit to discuss and co-ordinate international policies.
Hardcore unemployment
T3 · 2026-11-13		People who have been out of work for a long time and find it very hard to get and keep a job because of personal barriers, such as poor health, disability or a lack of basic skills. They are unlikely to be hired even when jobs are available.
Industrial relations commission
T3 · 2026-11-13		Industrial relations commissions or tribunals are agencies that historically have been set up by the federal or state governments to consider issues such as industrial dispute resolution, award determination and enterprise agreement registration. At the national level this role is now carried out by the Fair Work Commission; some states still have their own industrial relations commission.
Kyoto Protocol
T3 · 2026-11-13		An international treaty on climate change, adopted in 1997 and in force from 2005, that set binding targets for developed countries to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions. Australia ratified it in 2007. It has been superseded by the Paris Agreement (2015), under which every country sets its own emissions target.
NAFTA
T1 · 2026-11-13		The North American Free Trade Agreement was a free trade agreement between Canada, the USA and Mexico that began in 1994. It was replaced by the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) on 1 July 2020. See USMCA.
Trade Practices Act
T4 · 2026-11-13		The Trade Practices Act 1974 was Australia's main competition and consumer protection law until 1 January 2011, when it was replaced by the Competition and Consumer Act 2010. It covered anti-competitive and unfair market practices, mergers and acquisitions of companies, product safety and liability, and third-party access to facilities of national significance.
