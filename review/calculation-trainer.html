Real growth rate (T1)

Formula as shown

  • Real growth rate = (Real GDP in given year − Real GDP in previous year) ÷ Real GDP in previous year × 100

Question wording

  • Real GDP in a hypothetical economy: […] Calculate the real growth rate from Year 1 to Year 2.

Watch out for

  • Divide by the earlier year, not the later one.

Real GDP per capita (T1)

Formula as shown

  • Real GDP per capita = Total real GDP ÷ Total population

Question wording

  • An economy has real GDP of $[…] billion and a population of […] million . Calculate real GDP per capita.

Watch out for

  • Put both figures in the same units first: a billion is 1,000 million.

Real GDP and real growth from nominal GDP (T3)

Formula as shown

  • Real GDP = Money (nominal) GDP × CPI in base year ÷ CPI in given year
  • Then: real growth rate = change in real GDP ÷ real GDP in previous year × 100

Question wording

  • In Year 2 an economy has nominal GDP of $[…] billion . The CPI was 100 in the base year and is […] in Year 2. Calculate real GDP in Year 2.
  • Data for a hypothetical economy: […] Calculate the rate of real economic growth from Year 1 to Year 2.

Watch out for

  • Deflating removes the effect of price rises, so real GDP must be smaller than nominal GDP when the CPI is above 100.
  • The trap is to use nominal GDP: that would give […]%, which includes inflation.

Tariff: imports and revenue (T1)

Formula as shown

  • Imports = Quantity demanded − Quantity supplied by domestic producers
  • Tariff revenue = Tariff per unit × Quantity of imports after the tariff

Question wording

    Watch out for

    • Imports fall from both sides: domestic supply rises and domestic demand falls.
    • Use the row with the tariff. Under free trade imports were […] million.
    • Use imports after the tariff. Using free-trade imports ([…] million) gives $[…] million, the most common error.

    Current account balance (T2)

    Formula as shown

    • Net goods + Net services = Balance on goods and services (BOGS)
    • Balance on current account = BOGS + Net primary income + Net secondary income

    Question wording

    • Balance of payments data for an economy ($ billion): […] Calculate net secondary income.
    • Balance of payments data for an economy ($ billion): […] Calculate the balance on goods and services (BOGS).
    • Balance of payments data for an economy ($ billion): […] Calculate the balance on current account.

    Watch out for

    • Rearrange the current account formula, and take care with the negative signs.
    • BOGS leaves out the two income items.
    • Net primary income is usually negative for Australia: add it with its sign.

    Balance of payments identity (T2)

    Formula as shown

    • Current account + Capital and financial account = 0 (ignoring net errors and omissions)
    • Capital and financial account = Balance on capital account + Balance on financial account

    Question wording

    • An economy records a balance on current account of […] ($ billion). Net errors and omissions are zero. What is the balance on the capital and financial account?
    • Balance of payments data ($ billion). Net errors and omissions are zero. […] Calculate the balance on the capital account.
    • Balance of payments data ($ billion). Net errors and omissions are zero. […] Calculate the balance on the financial account.

    Watch out for

    • A deficit on one account is offset by an equal surplus on the other.
    • The capital account is small. The financial account does most of the offsetting.
    • The three balances must sum to zero.

    Net foreign liabilities (T3)

    Formula as shown

    • Net foreign liabilities = Net foreign debt + Net foreign equity
    • As a percentage of GDP = Net foreign liabilities ÷ GDP × 100

    Question wording

    • An economy has net foreign liabilities of $[…] billion and net foreign debt of $[…] billion . Calculate net foreign equity.
    • Data for an economy ($ billion): […] Calculate net foreign liabilities as a percentage of GDP.
    • An economy has net foreign debt of $[…] billion and net foreign equity of […] billion . Calculate net foreign liabilities.

    Watch out for

    • Liabilities are debt plus equity.
    • Add debt and equity first, then divide by GDP.
    • Debt is what is owed; equity is what is owned.

    Exchange rate changes (T2)

    Formula as shown

    • Percentage change = (New rate − Old rate) ÷ Old rate × 100
    • Value in A$ = Value in US$ ÷ Exchange rate (US$ per A$)

    Question wording

    • An Australian exporter has a contract worth US$[…] million . The exchange rate moves from A$1 = US$[…] to A$1 = US$[…] . Calculate the value of the contract in Australian dollars at the new exchange rate.
    • The Australian dollar moves from US$[…] to US$[…] . Calculate the percentage change in the exchange rate.

    Watch out for

    • Divide by the rate when it is quoted as US$ per A$1. An appreciation lowers the A$ value of export income.
    • Divide by the old rate. Dividing by the new rate gives […]%, which is wrong.

    The simple multiplier (T3)

    Formula as shown

    • MPC = Change in consumption ÷ Change in income
    • MPS = Change in savings ÷ Change in income
    • MPC + MPS = 1
    • k = 1 ÷ (1 − MPC) or 1 ÷ MPS
    • Change in income (ΔY) = Change in injection × k

    Question wording

    • In a hypothetical economy the marginal propensity to consume (MPC) is […] . Calculate the simple multiplier.
    • Calculate the simple multiplier for an economy in which the marginal propensity to save (MPS) is […] .
    • In a hypothetical economy the ${RNG() < 0.5 ?
    • When national income rises by $[…] million , ${give === 'c' ?
    • An economy has an MPC of […] . The government wants national income to rise by $[…] million . Calculate the increase in […] needed.
    • Government spending rises by $[…] million and, as a result, national income eventually rises by $[…] million . Calculate the multiplier and the MPC.

    Watch out for

    • Subtract the MPC from 1 before you divide.
    • With the MPS, divide 1 by it directly.
    • Find the multiplier first. Say whether income rises or falls.
    • MPC + MPS = 1, so one gives you the other.
    • Divide the target change in income by the multiplier.
    • Work backwards: multiplier first, then the MPS, then the MPC.

    Leakages and injections (T3)

    Formula as shown

    • Leakages = S + T + M
    • Injections = I + G + X
    • Leakages < Injections: the economy is expanding. Leakages > Injections: contracting
    • Budget: G > T is a deficit; G < T is a surplus
    • Trade balance = X − M

    Question wording

      Watch out for

      • G greater than T is a deficit, which is expansionary.
      • Exports are an injection and imports are a leakage.
      • Leakages take money out of the circular flow; injections put it in.

      Unemployment and participation rates (T3)

      Formula as shown

      • Labour force = Employed full-time + Employed part-time + Unemployed
      • Unemployment rate = Unemployed ÷ Labour force × 100
      • Participation rate = Labour force ÷ Working age population × 100

      Question wording

        Watch out for

        • The unemployed are part of the labour force. The working age population is not the labour force.
        • Include the unemployed in the labour force before dividing.
        • Divide by the labour force, not the working age population (that would give […]%).

        Inflation rate (T3)

        Formula as shown

        • Inflation rate = (Current CPI − Previous year’s CPI) ÷ Previous year’s CPI × 100
        • Contribution of an item = its weighting × its percentage price change

        Question wording

        • The consumer price index (CPI) is […] in Year 1 and […] in Year 2. Calculate the inflation rate between Year 1 and Year 2.
        • A simplified CPI has three expenditure groups. Each group's index was 100 last year. […] Calculate the inflation rate for the year.

        Watch out for

        • Weight each price change before adding. A simple average of the three changes ignores the weightings.
        • The answer is not the difference in index points ([…]) unless the earlier CPI is 100.

        Gini coefficient (T3)

        Formula as shown

        • Gini coefficient = A ÷ (A + B)
        • A is the area between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve; B is the area under the Lorenz curve
        • 0 is perfect equality; 1 is perfect inequality

        Question wording

        • On a Lorenz curve diagram, area A (between the line of equality and the Lorenz curve) is […] square units and area B (under the Lorenz curve) is […] square units. Calculate the Gini coefficient.
        • Shares of total income by household quintile: […] Calculate the ratio of the highest quintile's share to the lowest quintile's share.

        Watch out for

        • A higher ratio means a less equal distribution.
        • Divide by A + B, not by B (that would give […]).

        Budget outcome (T4)

        Formula as shown

        • Budget outcome = Revenue − Expenditure
        • Positive is a surplus; negative is a deficit
        • As a percentage of GDP = Budget outcome ÷ GDP × 100

        Question wording

        • Data for a hypothetical economy ($ billion): […] Calculate the budget outcome as a percentage of GDP.
        • In a hypothetical economy government revenue is $[…] billion and government expenditure is $[…] billion . Calculate the budget outcome.

        Watch out for

        • Find the outcome in dollars first, then divide by GDP.
        • A […]: say so if the question asks for the stance.
