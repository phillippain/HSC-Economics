What is in here. Section I covers 2009–2025 (17 papers); Sections II, III and IV cover 2002–2025. The 2001 and 2009 extended-response papers are missing from the public archive, and the 2009 entries that do appear are reconstructed from that year's marking guidelines. A thin result can mean a real gap in examining, or it can mean a gap in the archive — the year range above tells you which.

How questions are matched. Each multiple-choice question and each short-answer sub-part is filed against the single dot point it turns on most directly. Section III and IV questions are matched to several dot points, because a 20-mark answer genuinely draws on several.

Every question is here in full. Multiple choice shows the stem, any diagram or table, and the four options, exactly as the paper set them. The one-line summary of what a question examined sits behind the reveal with the answer, because for many questions that summary names the correct option. Searching still reads it, so a search for a term that appears only in the summary will still find the question.

Marking criteria. Criteria, sample answers and the “answers could include” content are taken from the NESA marking guidelines for 2009 to 2024. They unfold in stages — criteria first, then the sample answer — so you can attempt a question before seeing how it is marked. Earlier years and 2025 have no criteria in the page; use the link to the guidelines instead.

Ticks are private to this device. They are kept in your own browser and are not sent anywhere. They will disappear if you clear site data or open the page on a different device or browser.
