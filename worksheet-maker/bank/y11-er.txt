=== er y11-er-P1-choice
c: P1a.2 P1a.3 P1a.4 P1a.5 P1c.1
lv: 2
k: explain
Q: Explain how the economic problem and opportunity cost shape the decisions made by consumers, producers and governments.
PTS:
- Defines the economic problem (unlimited wants, scarce resources) and opportunity cost accurately
- Consumers: choices about spending and saving, decided at the margin, with examples
- Producers: what and how much to produce and how to combine resources, with examples
- Governments: choices between spending priorities, with a production possibilities frontier showing the trade-off
- Uses economic terms (marginal cost and benefit, trade-offs, incentives) throughout
PLAN:
- Introduction: the economic problem and why every agent must choose
- Consumers: budgets, trade-offs and opportunity cost
- Producers: allocating scarce resources and the cost–benefit principle
- Governments: competing priorities, illustrated with a PPF
- Conclusion: opportunity cost is the common thread in all economic decisions

=== er y11-er-P1-cycle

lv: 3
k: analyse
Q: Explain the causes of fluctuations in the business cycle and analyse their effects on households, businesses and government.
PTS:
- Describes the stages of the business cycle, using a diagram
- Causes: changes in injections and leakages (e.g. investment, exports, confidence, interest rates), shocks such as pandemics or global downturns
- Effects on households: jobs, incomes, wealth and confidence
- Effects on businesses: sales, profits, investment and hiring; on government: tax revenue, welfare spending and the budget

- Introduction: the business cycle and why economies fluctuate
- Causes of fluctuations, using the circular flow of income
- Effects of a contraction on households, businesses and government
- Effects of an expansion, including inflation

=== er y11-er-P2-elasticity

lv: 3
k: analyse calc
Q: Analyse the significance of price elasticity of demand and price elasticity of supply for businesses and governments.
PTS:
- Defines PED and PES, the degrees of elasticity and the percentage method, with a calculation
- Factors affecting PED (necessity, substitutes, share of income, time, addictiveness) and PES (time, spare capacity, storage, mobility of factors)
- Significance for businesses: pricing and total revenue, product differentiation, responding to price rises
- Significance for governments: tax revenue and tax incidence, e.g. excise on tobacco or fuel
- Uses diagrams and examples throughout
PLAN:
- Introduction: elasticity defined
- PED: degrees, measurement and factors
- PED and business decisions on price and revenue
- PES and how businesses respond to price changes
- Governments: taxes and their incidence
- Conclusion
