# Year 11 (2025 syllabus) cause and effect chains: "- variable => up | why" or "- variable => down | why".

=== chain y11-ch-P2c-frost
c: P2c.4 P2d.1 P2b.4
lv: 1
TITLE: Frost hits the coffee crop
TRIGGER: Frost destroys a large share of the coffee crop in a major growing country.
- The supply of coffee => down | Less coffee is available at every price
- The world price of coffee => up | At the old price there is a shortage, so the price rises
- The quantity of coffee bought => down | Buyers contract their purchases at the higher price
- Café prices for a cup of coffee => up | Cafés pass on the higher cost of beans
- The demand for tea => up | Tea is a substitute, so some coffee drinkers switch

=== chain y11-ch-P2i-excise
c: P2i.2 P2e.4 P5b.5
lv: 2
TITLE: A higher tobacco excise
TRIGGER: The government raises the excise tax on each packet of cigarettes.
- Producers' costs per packet => up | The tax is paid on each packet sold
- The supply of cigarettes => down | Higher costs shift the supply curve up by the tax
- The price consumers pay => up | Producers pass most of the tax on because demand is inelastic
- The quantity of cigarettes bought => down | Some smokers cut back or quit, though only a little in the short run
- Tax revenue => up | Demand is inelastic, so sales fall less than the tax per packet rises
- Smoking-related health costs over time => down | Fewer people smoke, a smaller negative externality

=== chain y11-ch-P4c-rates
c: P4c.3 P4c.7 P4c.4
lv: 2
TITLE: Interest rates rise
TRIGGER: Banks raise interest rates on loans and deposits.
- The cost of borrowing => up | Interest is the price of credit
- Borrowing for housing and business investment => down | Fewer projects and purchases are worth the higher cost
- The reward for saving => up | Deposits earn more interest
- Mortgage repayments for existing borrowers => up | Variable-rate loans cost more each month
- Housing affordability for new buyers => down | Buyers can borrow less for the same repayment
- Household spending => down | Borrowers have less left over and savers have a reason to save

=== chain y11-ch-P3a-thrift
c: P3a.6 P1d.5 P1e.6
lv: 3
TITLE: Everyone saves more: the paradox of thrift
TRIGGER: Worried about the future, all households decide to save a larger share of their income.
- Household consumption => down | Saving is income that is not spent
- Leakages from the circular flow => up | Saving is a leakage
- Business sales => down | Firms sell less to households
- Production and employment => down | Firms cut output and jobs
- National income => down | Lower production means lower incomes, so households may end up saving no more than before
- Business investment => down | With weaker sales, firms need less new equipment, so spending falls further

=== chain y11-ch-P3c-tech
c: P3c.4 P3c.5 P2c.4 P1c.2
lv: 2
TITLE: A productivity breakthrough
TRIGGER: A new technology lets factories produce much more with the same workers and machines.
- Productivity => up | More output from the same inputs
- Costs of producing each unit => down | Fixed costs are spread over more output
- Supply => up | Firms will supply more at every price
- Prices of the goods => down | The supply curve shifts right
- Real wages and living standards => up | Higher output per worker allows higher real incomes
- The economy's production possibilities => up | The frontier shifts outwards

=== chain y11-ch-P1e-exports
c: P1e.6 P1e.7 P1d.5 P6a.4
lv: 2
TITLE: A recession overseas
TRIGGER: Australia's major trading partners fall into recession.
- Demand for Australian exports => down | Overseas buyers spend less
- Injections into the circular flow => down | Exports are an injection
- Real GDP growth in Australia => down | Lower spending means lower production
- Unemployment => up | Firms need fewer workers
- Government tax revenue => down | Incomes and profits fall
- Government spending on unemployment benefits => up | More people need income support
