Q: Explain the relationship between scarcity and opportunity cost.
PTS:
- Scarcity: people's wants are unlimited but the resources to satisfy them are limited (1)
- So every individual, business and government must choose how to use resources (1)
- Each choice has an opportunity cost, the next best alternative forgone, e.g. a council that builds a pool cannot use the same funds for a library (1)

PTS:

- Example, e.g. a rebate on solar panels encourages households to install them; a tax on sugary drinks discourages buying them (1)
- Behavioural biases mean people do not always make the rational, self-interested choice that economic models assume (1)
- Example, e.g. present bias leads people to under-save for retirement; default bias keeps people in a high-fee super fund (1)

Q: Explain how market equilibrium contributes to an efficient allocation of resources.
PTS:
- At equilibrium the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied: no shortage or surplus (1)
- Every unit that buyers value at more than its cost of production is produced, and none that cost more than they are worth to buyers (1)
- So resources go to their most valued uses (allocative efficiency), and competition pushes firms to produce at least cost (1)

k: explain
Q: Explain why a negative externality in production leads to market failure. Refer to marginal private cost and marginal social cost.
PTS:
- Producers base supply on their marginal private costs (MPC) (1)
- A negative externality, e.g. pollution, imposes extra costs on third parties, so marginal social cost (MSC) is greater than MPC (1)
- The market therefore produces more than the socially efficient quantity, where MSC equals marginal social benefit (1)
- Resources are misallocated: too much of the good is produced at too low a price, a market failure (deadweight loss) (1)

Q: Explain the paradox of thrift.
PTS:
- Saving is deferred consumption; one household saving more can be sensible (1)
- If all households save more at the same time, total consumption falls (1)
- Firms sell less, cut production and jobs, so national income falls (1)
- With lower incomes, total saving may not rise at all: what is wise for one household can be harmful for the economy as a whole (1)
