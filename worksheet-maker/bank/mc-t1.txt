# New multiple-choice questions, Topic 1. ANS is the correct letter; WHY explains it.

=== mc mc-1A1-01
c: 1A.1
lv: 1
Q: Which of the following best describes the global economy?
A: The economies of the G20 countries
B: The sum of the world's national economies and the links between them through trade, finance and people
C: The total value of world exports in a year
D: The economies that belong to the World Trade Organization
ANS: B
WHY: The global economy is all the world's national economies together, linked through trade, financial flows, investment, technology and migration. Exports (C) are only one link; A and D are subsets of countries.

=== mc mc-1A2-01
c: 1A.2
lv: 2
Q: Gross World Product grew by 3.2 per cent in real terms in a year in which world population grew by 1 per cent. Which statement is correct?
A: Real output per person fell
B: Real output per person rose by about 2.2 per cent
C: Real output per person rose by about 4.2 per cent
D: Nominal output must have grown by less than 3.2 per cent
ANS: B
WHY: Growth in real output per person is roughly real growth minus population growth: 3.2 − 1.0 ≈ 2.2 per cent. Nominal output (D) would usually grow faster than real output because of inflation.

A: Higher shipping costs
B: Higher tariffs on manufactured goods
C: Lower tariffs and the spread of digital trade platforms

ANS: C
WHY: Lower trade barriers and cheaper ways to buy and sell across borders raise trade. Higher costs and barriers (A, B, D) reduce it.

Q: Which of the following is an example of a global financial flow?
A: An Australian winery exports wine to the United Kingdom
B: A Japanese pension fund buys Australian government bonds
C: A German tourist visits the Great Barrier Reef
D: A worker migrates from India to Australia
ANS: B
WHY: Buying bonds overseas is a movement of financial capital. Wine exports and tourism are trade in goods and services; migration is a movement of labour.

=== mc mc-1A4-02

Q: Why can a financial crisis in one country spread quickly to other economies?
A: Exchange rates are fixed between most major economies
B: Financial markets are closely integrated, so investors move funds and losses across borders very quickly
C: Most countries ban foreign investment during a crisis
D: Governments must keep their budgets balanced
ANS: B
WHY: Integrated financial markets let capital move instantly, so falling asset prices, losses and loss of confidence in one market spread to others (contagion), as in the 2008 global financial crisis.

=== mc mc-1A8-01

Q: A sharp slowdown in China would most likely affect Australia through
A: higher demand for Australian iron ore.
B: lower export volumes and prices, and a lower Australian dollar.
C: a higher Australian terms of trade.
D: lower unemployment in Australian mining regions.
ANS: B
WHY: China is Australia's largest export market. A slowdown reduces demand for resources, lowering export volumes and prices (the terms of trade fall) and the dollar, which spreads the downturn to Australia.

=== mc mc-1B1-01
c: 1B.1
lv: 2
Q: In one day, Country X can make 10 shirts or 5 bikes, and Country Y can make 6 shirts or 2 bikes. Which statement is correct?
A: Country X has a comparative advantage in shirts
B: Country Y has a comparative advantage in bikes
C: Country X has a comparative advantage in bikes and Country Y in shirts
D: Neither country can gain from trade, because Country X is better at both
ANS: C
WHY: For X, 1 bike costs 2 shirts; for Y, 1 bike costs 3 shirts. X makes bikes at a lower opportunity cost, so Y has the comparative advantage in shirts (1 shirt costs Y ⅓ bike but costs X ½ bike). Both gain from specialising, even though X has an absolute advantage in both.

=== mc mc-1B1-02
c: 1B.1
lv: 1
Q: Which of the following is a disadvantage of free trade for an economy?
A: Consumers can choose from a wider range of goods
B: Resources move to industries in which the economy is most efficient
C: Jobs may be lost in industries that cannot compete with imports
D: Firms can sell to larger markets and gain economies of scale
ANS: C
WHY: Free trade forces structural change: import-competing industries may shrink, causing job losses in the short term. A, B and D are advantages.

=== mc mc-1B2-01

Q: Which international organisation lends to countries with balance of payments difficulties, usually with conditions attached?
A: The World Trade Organization
B: The International Monetary Fund
C: The OECD
D: The G20
ANS: B
WHY: The IMF lends to countries facing balance of payments crises and sets conditions on their policies. The WTO deals with trade rules; the OECD and G20 are forums for research and coordination.

B: It is a forum where major advanced and emerging economies coordinate economic policy

=== mc mc-1B4-02

Q: Australia signs a free trade agreement with Country Z, and Australians switch from buying cheaper TVs from Country Q to slightly dearer TVs from Country Z. This is an example of
A: trade creation.
B: trade diversion.
C: dumping.
D: comparative advantage.
ANS: B
WHY: Imports have switched from the lowest-cost producer (Q) to a higher-cost member of the agreement (Z) because Z no longer faces a tariff: trade diversion.

=== mc mc-1C1-01

lv: 1
Q: Which argument for protection is most widely accepted by economists?
A: Protection keeps all domestic jobs safe
B: Temporary help for an infant industry with real potential to become competitive
C: Protection improves the trade balance permanently
D: Protection lowers prices for consumers
ANS: B
WHY: The infant industry argument has some support if the help is temporary and the industry can become competitive. Protection raises prices (D is wrong) and does not save jobs overall (A).

=== mc mc-1C2-01
c: 1C.2
lv: 2
Q: Compared with a tariff that gives domestic producers the same price, an import quota
A: raises more revenue for the government.
B: lets importers keep the extra revenue from the higher price.
C: has no deadweight loss.
D: lowers the price consumers pay.
ANS: B
WHY: A quota and an equivalent tariff raise the price by the same amount, but with a quota the government collects nothing: the importers who hold the quota keep the extra revenue. Both have deadweight losses.

=== mc mc-1C2-02
c: 1C.2
lv: 2
Q: A production subsidy to an import-competing industry will
A: raise the price consumers pay.
B: reduce domestic production.
C: increase domestic production while consumers still pay the world price.
D: raise revenue for the government.
ANS: C
WHY: The subsidy shifts domestic supply right. Consumers keep paying the world price, domestic production rises and imports fall, and the government bears the cost.

Q: Strong growth in China and India since the 1990s has most likely
A: increased inequality between countries but reduced it within every country.
B: reduced inequality between countries, while inequality within some of those countries rose.
C: had no effect on the global distribution of income.
D: reduced the number of people living in extreme poverty in Africa only.
ANS: B
WHY: Rapid growth in large, poorer economies lifted hundreds of millions out of poverty and narrowed the gap between countries. Within China, inequality rose as coastal and urban incomes grew faster.
