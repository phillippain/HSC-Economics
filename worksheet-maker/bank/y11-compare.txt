=== compare y11-cp-P1b-systems
c: P1b.3 P1b.4
lv: 2
Q: Compare a planned economy and a free market economy.
COLS: Planned economy | Free market economy
- Who decides what to produce => !A central planning authority (government) | Consumers, through their spending and the price mechanism
- Ownership of resources => Mostly owned by the state | Mostly privately owned
- How prices are set => Set by the government | By demand and supply in markets
- Incentive to produce => Meeting the plan's targets | The profit motive
- A strength => Can direct resources to national priorities and reduce inequality | Responds quickly to consumers and encourages efficiency and innovation
- A weakness => Shortages and surpluses; weak incentives to innovate | Inequality; public goods and merit goods under-provided

=== compare y11-cp-P1a-micro

lv: 1
Q: Compare microeconomics and macroeconomics.
COLS: Microeconomics | Macroeconomics
- What it studies => !Individual consumers, firms and markets | The economy as a whole
- Typical questions => How is the price of rental housing set? | Why has unemployment risen?
- Key measures => Prices, quantities and costs in one market | GDP, unemployment, inflation, the balance of payments
- Example of a policy => A tax on sugary drinks | A change in government spending to support jobs

=== compare y11-cp-P2g-structures
c: P2g.2
lv: 2
Q: Compare perfect competition and monopoly.
COLS: Perfect competition | Monopoly
- Number of sellers => !Very many | One
- Type of product => Identical (homogeneous) | Unique, with no close substitutes
- Barriers to entry => None | Very high
- Pricing power => Price taker: sells at the market price | Price maker: sets its own price
- Example => A market for one variety of wheat | The only water supplier in a city

=== compare y11-cp-P2h-extern
c: P2h.3 P2h.4
lv: 2
Q: Compare negative and positive externalities.
COLS: Negative externality | Positive externality
- Effect on third parties => !A cost imposed on others | A benefit enjoyed by others
- Social compared with private => Marginal social cost is greater than marginal private cost | Marginal social benefit is greater than marginal private benefit
- Market outcome => Too much produced or consumed | Too little produced or consumed
- Example => Pollution from a coal-fired power station | Vaccinations reducing the spread of disease
- A policy to correct it => A tax, regulation or tradeable permits | A subsidy or free provision

=== compare y11-cp-P2h-goods
c: P2h.5
lv: 2
Q: Compare public goods and private goods.
COLS: Public goods | Private goods
- Rivalry => !Non-rival: one person's use does not reduce what is left for others | Rival: one person's use leaves less for others
- Excludability => Non-excludable: people who do not pay cannot be stopped from using it | Excludable: people who do not pay can be kept out
- Free rider problem => Yes, so markets under-provide them | No
- Who usually provides them => Government | Private firms through markets
- Example => Street lighting, national defence | A sandwich, a mobile phone

=== compare y11-cp-P3c-profit
c: P3c.2
lv: 2
Q: Compare accounting profit and economic profit.
COLS: Accounting profit | Economic profit
- Definition => !Revenue minus explicit costs | Revenue minus explicit and implicit (opportunity) costs
- Costs included => Payments the business actually makes, e.g. wages, rent, materials | Those costs plus the income the owner's time and money could earn elsewhere
- Usually larger or smaller? => Larger | Smaller
- What it tells the owner => Whether the business makes money on paper | Whether staying in business is better than the next best use of the owner's resources

=== compare y11-cp-P4-markets
c: P4b.1 P4c.1
lv: 2
Q: Compare equity markets and debt markets.
COLS: Equity markets | Debt markets
- What is traded => !Shares (part-ownership of companies) | Bonds and other loans
- What the investor receives => Dividends and capital gains (or losses) | Interest, and the amount lent is repaid
- Risk for the investor => Higher: returns depend on the company's profits | Lower: payments are set, unless the borrower defaults
- Effect on the business => Raises funds without borrowing, but shares control | Raises funds that must be repaid with interest
- Example => A company floating on the ASX | The Australian Government issuing bonds

=== compare y11-cp-P5b-tax
c: P5b.5 P5b.4
lv: 2
Q: Compare direct and indirect taxes.
COLS: Direct taxes | Indirect taxes
- What is taxed => !Income, profits or wealth | Spending on goods and services
- Who pays the tax office => The person or business that earns the income | Businesses, which pass the cost on in prices
- Effect on fairness => Can be progressive, taking more from those with higher incomes | Usually regressive, taking a larger share of low incomes
- Examples => Personal income tax, company tax | GST, fuel and tobacco excise
