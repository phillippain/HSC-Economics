# Year 11 (2025 syllabus) multiple choice: Focus area 1, Introduction to economics.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-01
c: P1a.2
lv: 1
Q: The basic economic problem arises because
A: resources are unlimited but people's wants are limited
B: people's wants are unlimited but the resources to satisfy them are scarce
C: governments control most of the economy's resources
D: prices are too high for many consumers
ANS: B
WHY: Scarcity, unlimited wants and limited resources, forces every economy to make choices. A reverses the problem; C and D describe particular situations, not the basic problem.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-02
c: P1a.4
lv: 1
Q: Kate could spend Saturday at the beach or work a shift that pays $160. She chooses to work. What is the opportunity cost of working?
A: The $160 she earns
B: The cost of travelling to work
C: Nothing, because she is paid
D: The day at the beach she gives up
ANS: D
WHY: Opportunity cost is the next best alternative given up when a choice is made, here the day at the beach. The $160 is the benefit of working, not its cost.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-03
c: P1a.3
lv: 1
Q: Which of the following is an economic decision made by a producer?
A: A family deciding whether to buy a new car
B: A bakery deciding how many loaves to bake each morning
C: The Federal Government deciding how much to spend on hospitals
D: A student deciding whether to save her part-time earnings
ANS: B
WHY: Producers (businesses) decide what and how much to produce. A and D are consumer decisions; C is a government decision.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-04
c: P1a.5
lv: 2
k: calc
Q: A café owner is deciding whether to stay open one extra hour each evening. The extra hour would bring in $300 of sales and add $220 to wages and other costs. Using the cost–benefit principle at the margin, she should
A: stay open, because the marginal benefit ($300) is greater than the marginal cost ($220)
B: close, because the café already makes a profit during the day
C: stay open only if the café's total revenue for the day is above its total cost
D: close, because wages are a cost of production
ANS: A
WHY: A decision at the margin compares the extra benefit with the extra cost of that one choice. The extra hour adds $80 to profit, so it is worth doing; the rest of the day's results (B, C) do not change that.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-05
c: P1a.6
lv: 2
Q: A council gives a $50 rebate to households that buy a water-saving shower head. Some of these households then take longer showers. This is an example of
A: an intended consequence of a disincentive
B: a market reaching equilibrium
C: the opportunity cost of saving water
D: an unintended consequence of an incentive
ANS: D
WHY: The rebate is an incentive to save water; longer showers are a response the council did not intend, which reduces the water saved.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-06
c: P1a.9
lv: 2
Q: Many people keep the superannuation fund their employer chose for them, even when another fund would charge lower fees and leave them better off. Behavioural economists explain this mainly as
A: rational, self-interested behaviour
B: the law of diminishing marginal utility
C: status quo (default) bias
D: a response to perfect information
ANS: C
WHY: People tend to stick with the default option even when switching would benefit them, which challenges the assumption that people always act rationally in their own interest.

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-07
c: P1a.10
lv: 1
Q: Which of the following is a macroeconomic issue?
A: The price of avocados at a local market
B: The wage paid to a nurse
C: Australia's unemployment rate
D: A firm's decision to hire one more worker
ANS: C
WHY: Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole, such as total output, unemployment and inflation. A, B and D concern individual markets, workers or firms (microeconomics).

=== mc y11-mc-P1a-08
c: P1a.8
lv: 2
Q: Economic models such as demand and supply often assume ceteris paribus. This means that
A: the government does not intervene in markets
B: consumers have perfect information
C: all other factors are held constant while one factor changes
D: prices never change
ANS: C
WHY: Ceteris paribus ("other things equal") lets a model show the effect of one change at a time. It is a simplifying assumption, not a claim that nothing else ever changes.

=== mc y11-mc-P1b-01
c: P1b.2
lv: 1
Q: Which factor income is the reward for entrepreneurship?
A: Rent
B: Wages
C: Interest
D: Profit
ANS: D
WHY: Entrepreneurs organise the other factors and take the risk, and their reward is profit. Rent is paid for land, wages for labour and interest for capital.

A: Land (natural resources)
B: Labour
C: Capital

=== mc y11-mc-P1b-03
c: P1b.4
lv: 2
Q: In a planned (command) economy, the question "What to produce?" is mainly answered by
A: consumers, through the price mechanism
B: government planners
C: tradition and custom
D: competition between private firms
ANS: B
WHY: In a planned economy the state owns most resources and a central authority decides what is produced. A and D describe a free market economy; C describes a pre-industrial (traditional) economy.

=== mc y11-mc-P1b-04

Q: Australia is best described as a mixed market economy because
A: the government owns all the major industries
B: prices are set by a central planning authority
C: production is decided mainly by tradition
D: most resources are allocated by markets, while the government provides some goods and services and regulates markets
ANS: D
WHY: A mixed economy combines market allocation with a significant role for government, for example health, education, welfare and regulation in Australia.

A: is accepted in exchange for goods and services

C: can be saved and spent later

=== mc y11-mc-P1c-01
c: P1c.4 P1c.1
lv: 2
k: calc table
Q: The production possibilities schedule shows an economy that produces only food and machines. What is the opportunity cost of increasing machine production from 20 to 30 units?
TABLE: Production possibilities schedule
| Point | Food (tonnes) | Machines (units) |
| A | 100 | 0 |
| B | 90 | 10 |
| C | 75 | 20 |
| D | 55 | 30 |
| E | 30 | 40 |
| F | 0 | 50 |
A: 10 tonnes of food
B: 20 tonnes of food
C: 55 tonnes of food
D: 10 machines
ANS: B
WHY: Moving from C to D, food falls from 75 to 55 tonnes, so the 10 extra machines cost 20 tonnes of food (2 tonnes per machine).

=== mc y11-mc-P1c-02
c: P1c.5
lv: 1
Q: A point inside an economy's production possibilities frontier shows
A: a combination of goods that cannot be produced with current resources
B: full and efficient use of all resources
C: unemployed or underemployed resources
D: economic growth
ANS: C
WHY: Inside the frontier the economy could produce more of one good without giving up any of the other, so some resources are idle or not used efficiently. A describes a point outside the frontier.

=== mc y11-mc-P1c-03
c: P1c.2 P1c.5
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following would shift an economy's production possibilities frontier outwards?
A: A fall in unemployment
B: A new technology that raises productivity in all industries
C: Moving resources from making consumer goods to making capital goods
D: A recession
ANS: B
WHY: Better technology lets the same resources produce more of both goods. A moves the economy from inside the frontier towards it; C is a movement along the frontier; D moves the economy inside it.

=== mc y11-mc-P1c-04
c: P1c.3
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is an assumption of the production possibilities model?
A: Prices are set by the government
B: Resources can grow without limit during the period
C: The economy produces two goods with a fixed quantity of resources and given technology
D: Consumers decide what is produced
ANS: C
WHY: The model holds resources and technology fixed and assumes they are fully used, so it can show the trade-off between two goods.

=== mc y11-mc-P1c-05
c: P1c.6
lv: 2
Q: An economy decides to produce more capital goods and fewer consumer goods this year. The most likely result is
A: no opportunity cost, because capital goods are useful
B: a point inside the production possibilities frontier
C: a production possibilities frontier that shifts inwards
D: lower consumption now but a frontier that can shift outwards in the future
ANS: D
WHY: The opportunity cost is consumer goods today. More machinery and infrastructure raise future productive capacity, so the frontier can shift out later.

=== mc y11-mc-P1d-01
c: P1d.2
lv: 1
Q: Which of the following is an injection into the circular flow of income?
A: Savings
B: Taxation
C: Exports
D: Imports
ANS: C
WHY: Exports bring spending into the economy from overseas. Savings, taxation and imports are leakages from the circular flow.

=== mc y11-mc-P1d-02
c: P1d.4
lv: 2
k: calc
Q: In an economy, S = $60b, T = $90b, M = $50b, I = $70b, G = $85b and X = $55b. This economy is
A: in equilibrium
B: in disequilibrium, and national income will fall
C: in disequilibrium, and national income will rise
D: in equilibrium, because government spending is less than taxation
ANS: C
WHY: Leakages S + T + M = $200b and injections I + G + X = $210b. Injections exceed leakages, so spending and income rise until the two are equal.

=== mc y11-mc-P1d-03
c: P1d.1
lv: 1
Q: In the circular flow of income, households provide businesses with
A: goods and services
B: taxes
C: profits
D: the factors of production: land, labour, capital and enterprise
ANS: D
WHY: Households own the factors of production and sell their services to businesses in return for factor incomes. Businesses provide goods and services to households.

=== mc y11-mc-P1d-04
c: P1d.5
lv: 2
Q: Households decide to save a larger share of their income. Other things equal, the circular flow model predicts that
A: leakages fall and national income rises
B: injections rise and national income rises
C: leakages rise and national income falls
D: there is no effect, because savings stay in the economy
ANS: C
WHY: Saving is a leakage: money saved is not spent on goods and services, so spending and income fall unless the savings are matched by more investment.

=== mc y11-mc-P1d-05
c: P1d.1
lv: 2
Q: Which sector's main role in the circular flow of income is to channel savings to businesses for investment?
A: The international sector
B: The government sector
C: The financial sector
D: The household sector
ANS: C
WHY: Banks, super funds and other financial institutions take in savings and lend or invest them, linking a leakage (S) with an injection (I).

=== mc y11-mc-P1e-01
c: P1e.4
lv: 1
Q: The stage of the business cycle at which real GDP stops rising and begins to fall is the
A: trough
B: upturn
C: expansion
D: peak
ANS: D
WHY: The peak is the high point of the cycle, after which a contraction begins. The trough is the low point, after which an expansion (upturn) begins.

=== mc y11-mc-P1e-02
c: P1e.5
lv: 2
Q: During the contraction (downturn) phase of the business cycle, we would usually expect
A: unemployment to fall and inflation to rise
B: both unemployment and inflation to fall
C: unemployment to rise and inflationary pressure to ease
D: business profits to rise
ANS: C
WHY: Spending and production slow, so firms need fewer workers and have less ability to raise prices.

=== mc y11-mc-P1e-03
c: P1e.1
lv: 2
k: calc
Q: An economy's real GDP rose from $2,000 billion to $2,050 billion over a year. Its rate of economic growth was
A: 2.5 per cent
B: 5.0 per cent
C: 50 per cent
D: 2.4 per cent
ANS: A
WHY: Growth = (2,050 − 2,000) ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 2.5 per cent. D divides by the new value instead of the original one.

=== mc y11-mc-P1e-04
c: P1e.6
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following is most likely to start a downturn in Australia's business cycle?
A: A rise in business confidence
B: A large fall in exports because major trading partners are in recession
C: Lower interest rates
D: Rising household wealth
ANS: B
WHY: Exports are an injection; a large fall reduces spending, production and income. A, C and D would usually lift spending.

B: increase inflation
