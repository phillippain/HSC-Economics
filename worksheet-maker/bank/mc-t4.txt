# New multiple-choice questions, Topic 4.

=== mc mc-4A1-01
c: 4A.1
lv: 2
Q: Which statement about economic growth and quality of life is correct?
A: Higher real GDP always means a higher quality of life
B: Growth can raise living standards, but quality of life also depends on health, leisure, the environment and how income is shared
C: Quality of life is measured by the inflation rate
D: Governments do not aim for economic growth
ANS: B
WHY: Growth gives more goods, services and jobs, but quality of life also depends on non-material factors such as health, leisure, the environment and the distribution of income.

=== mc mc-4A2-01
c: 4A.2
lv: 2
Q: The objective of full employment is best described as
A: zero unemployment.
B: an unemployment rate at about the NAIRU, where only frictional, seasonal and some structural unemployment remain.
C: a participation rate of 100 per cent.
D: everyone working full-time.
ANS: B
WHY: Full employment allows for people moving between jobs and some mismatch; pushing unemployment below the NAIRU adds to inflation.

=== mc mc-4A3-01
c: 4A.3
lv: 1
Q: Price stability in Australia means
A: no change in any prices.
B: inflation of 2 to 3 per cent on average, the RBA's target.
C: falling prices each year.
D: inflation of 5 per cent.
ANS: B
WHY: The RBA targets inflation of 2 to 3 per cent, aiming for the midpoint. Low, positive inflation is the goal, not zero or falling prices.

=== mc mc-4A4-01
c: 4A.4
lv: 2
Q: The objective of external stability is best described as
A: never running a current account deficit.
B: keeping the current account deficit and foreign liabilities at levels that can be serviced, and avoiding big swings in the exchange rate.
C: fixing the exchange rate.
D: having more exports than imports every year.
ANS: B
WHY: External stability means Australia can meet its obligations to the rest of the world over time, so investors stay confident. A deficit is not a problem in itself if it is sustainable.

A: Subsidies for coal-fired power
B: A market-based price on emissions such as the Safeguard Mechanism's tradeable credits

=== mc mc-4A7-01

A: Price stability and environmental sustainability
B: Economic growth and external stability
C: Full employment and economic growth
D: Distribution of income and price stability
ANS: B
WHY: Faster growth raises spending on imports, widening the current account deficit: a conflict between growth and external stability.

=== mc mc-4B1-01
c: 4B.1
lv: 2
Q: Which policy mix is most suitable when inflation is well above target and unemployment is low?
A: Expansionary fiscal and expansionary monetary policy
B: Contractionary monetary policy, supported by a tighter budget
C: Cuts to the cash rate and tax cuts
D: Higher tariffs
ANS: B
WHY: With the economy running above capacity, contractionary policy slows demand. Fiscal and monetary policy working in the same direction makes the RBA's job easier.

=== mc mc-4C1-01

Q: Government revenue is $700 billion and government spending is $735 billion. The budget outcome is
A: a surplus of $35 billion.
B: a deficit of $35 billion.
C: a deficit of $1,435 billion.
D: balanced.
ANS: B
WHY: Budget outcome = revenue − spending = 700 − 735 = −$35 billion, a deficit.

=== mc mc-4C1-02

Q: The budget deficit widens because unemployment rises in a downturn, with no change in policy. This change is
A: an increase in the structural deficit.
B: an increase in the cyclical component of the budget, through automatic stabilisers.
C: discretionary expansionary fiscal policy.
D: a contractionary budget stance.
ANS: B
WHY: Lower tax receipts and higher welfare payments in a downturn change the cyclical component automatically. The structural balance reflects policy decisions.

=== mc mc-4D3-01
c: 4D.3 2C.2
lv: 2
Q: The RBA cuts the cash rate while overseas central banks keep theirs unchanged. Which outcome is most likely?
A: An appreciation of the A$ and lower inflation
B: A depreciation of the A$, higher spending and more inflationary pressure
C: Higher mortgage rates and lower spending
D: Lower investment and a fall in employment
ANS: B
WHY: Lower rates reduce the return on Australian assets, so the dollar tends to depreciate. Cheaper borrowing and a lower dollar lift spending and net exports, adding to activity and inflation.

=== mc mc-4D3-02
c: 4D.3 4H.1
lv: 3
Q: Why might a rise in the cash rate take a long time to lower inflation?
A: Banks never pass on changes in the cash rate
B: Households and firms take time to adjust spending, and many loans have fixed rates for a while
C: The RBA can only change the cash rate once a year
D: Higher rates immediately raise import prices
ANS: B
WHY: Monetary policy works with long and variable lags: borrowers on fixed rates are affected only when their loans roll over, and spending plans change slowly.

A: Policies that lift national saving and international competitiveness
B: Larger budget deficits funded by overseas borrowing
