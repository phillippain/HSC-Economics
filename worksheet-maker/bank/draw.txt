Q: The Federal Government cuts its spending and raises taxes to return the budget to surplus. Draw an AD–AS diagram to show the effect on the economy.

=== draw dr-tar-01
c: 1C.2 2D.3
lv: 2
DIA: tariff good=steel
Q: The government imposes a tariff on imported steel. Draw a diagram of the domestic steel market to show the effects of the tariff.
ANS: The domestic price rises from the world price Pw to Pw+t. Domestic production rises from Q1 to Q2 and consumption falls from Q4 to Q3, so imports fall from Q1–Q4 to Q2–Q3. The government collects revenue (the shaded rectangle), producers gain surplus, consumers lose surplus, and the two shaded triangles are deadweight losses.

=== draw dr-quo-01
c: 1C.2
lv: 3
DIA: quota good=cheese
Q: The government limits imports of cheese with a quota. Draw a diagram of the domestic cheese market to show the effects of the quota.
ANS: With imports limited to the quota, the domestic price rises from Pw to P1 until the gap between domestic demand (Q3) and domestic supply (Q2) equals the quota. Domestic production rises from Q1 to Q2 and consumption falls from Q4 to Q3. Unlike a tariff, the government collects no revenue: the importers keep the extra revenue from the higher price (the shaded rectangle). The two triangles are deadweight losses.

=== draw dr-ext-01
c: 3F.2
lv: 2
DIA: extern good=electricity
Q: Coal-fired power stations emit carbon dioxide and no policy makes them pay for the damage. Draw a diagram to show why the market produces too much electricity.
ANS: Producers base supply on their private costs (MPC), but the social cost (MSC) is higher by the external cost. The market produces Q_m where demand meets MPC, more than the socially efficient Qs where demand meets MSC, and the price is too low. The shaded triangle is the deadweight loss: market failure.

=== draw dr-ext-02

ANS: The carbon price makes producers pay the full social cost, so supply shifts up from MPC to MSC. Output falls from Q_m to the socially efficient Qs and the price rises from P_m to Ps: the externality is internalised and the deadweight loss disappears.

Q: Expansionary policy pushes unemployment below the NAIRU. Draw a Phillips curve diagram to show the short-run effect.
ANS: The economy moves up along the short-run Phillips curve from A to B: unemployment falls from U1 to U2 and inflation rises from π1 to π2. This shows the conflict between the objectives of full employment and price stability.

=== draw dr-phil-02
c: 4A.7 3C.3
lv: 3
DIA: phillips right
Q: After several years of high inflation, workers and firms expect prices to keep rising. Draw a Phillips curve diagram to show the effect of higher inflationary expectations.
ANS: Higher expectations are built into wage claims and price setting, so the Phillips curve shifts right (up) from PC1 to PC2. Each unemployment rate now comes with higher inflation; the economy may move from A to B with both inflation and unemployment higher. This is why central banks act early to keep expectations anchored.
