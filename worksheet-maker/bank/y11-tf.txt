# "- T: statement => (optional) why it is true"   "- F: false statement => the corrected statement"

=== tf y11-tf-P1a
c: P1a.1 P1a.2 P1a.3 P1a.4 P1a.5 P1a.6 P1a.9 P1a.10
- T: Scarcity exists because people's wants are unlimited while the resources to satisfy them are limited.
- F: Opportunity cost is the money price paid for a good. => Opportunity cost is the value of the next best alternative given up when a choice is made.
- T: Consumers, producers and governments are the economic agents that make economic decisions.
- F: The cost–benefit principle says a choice is worth making whenever its total benefit is positive. => The cost–benefit principle says a choice is worth making when its marginal benefit is at least as great as its marginal cost.
- T: An incentive can have unintended consequences that work against the aim of the policy.
- F: Behavioural economics assumes that people always make rational, self-interested decisions. => Behavioural economics challenges that assumption: cognitive biases and ethical concerns shape real decisions.
- T: Microeconomics studies individual markets, while macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole.

=== tf y11-tf-P1b
c: P1b.1 P1b.2 P1b.3 P1b.4 P1b.5
- T: The four factors of production are land (natural resources), labour, capital and entrepreneurship.
- F: Interest is the factor income earned by labour. => Wages are the income earned by labour; interest is the income earned by capital.
- T: Every economy must decide what to produce, how to produce, how much to produce and how to distribute production.
- F: In a free market economy, a central planning authority decides what is produced. => In a free market economy, consumers' spending and the price mechanism decide what is produced.
- T: Australia is a mixed market economy.
- F: Money acting as a store of value means it is accepted in exchange for goods and services. => That is money as a medium of exchange; as a store of value, money can be saved and spent later.

=== tf y11-tf-P1c
c: P1c.1 P1c.2 P1c.3 P1c.4 P1c.5 P1c.6
- T: A production possibilities frontier shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with its resources and technology.
- F: A point outside the production possibilities frontier shows unemployed resources. => A point inside the frontier shows unemployed resources; a point outside it cannot be reached with current resources.
- T: Moving along the frontier to produce more of one good has an opportunity cost: less of the other good.
- F: An improvement in technology shifts the production possibilities frontier inwards. => An improvement in technology shifts the frontier outwards.
- T: Producing more capital goods today can allow an economy to produce more of both goods in the future.

=== tf y11-tf-P1d

- F: Exports are a leakage from the circular flow of income. => Exports are an injection; imports are a leakage.

- F: When leakages are greater than injections, national income rises. => When leakages are greater than injections, national income falls.

=== tf y11-tf-P2a

=== tf y11-tf-P2b

- T: The law of demand states that, other things equal, a rise in price leads to a fall in the quantity demanded.
- F: A change in a good's own price shifts its demand curve. => A change in a good's own price causes a movement along the demand curve.
- T: A rise in the price of a substitute increases the demand for a good.
- F: A rise in the price of a complement increases the demand for a good. => A rise in the price of a complement decreases the demand for a good.

=== tf y11-tf-P2c

- T: The supply curve slopes upwards because higher prices make it profitable to produce more.

- T: A rise in the cost of the factors of production decreases supply.
- F: A rise in a good's own price is an increase in supply. => A rise in a good's own price causes an expansion of the quantity supplied, a movement along the curve.

=== tf y11-tf-P2d
c: P2d.1 P2d.2 P2d.3
- T: At equilibrium, the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied.
- F: When the price is above equilibrium, there is a shortage. => When the price is above equilibrium, there is a surplus.
- T: An increase in demand, with supply unchanged, raises both the equilibrium price and quantity.
- F: A decrease in supply lowers the equilibrium price. => A decrease in supply raises the equilibrium price and lowers the equilibrium quantity.

=== tf y11-tf-P2e
c: P2e.1 P2e.2 P2e.3 P2e.4 P2e.5

- F: When demand is price elastic, a price rise increases total revenue. => When demand is price elastic, a price rise reduces total revenue.
- T: Goods with few close substitutes, such as necessities, tend to have price inelastic demand.
- F: Demand usually becomes less elastic over time. => Demand usually becomes more elastic over time, as buyers find substitutes.
- T: A perfectly inelastic demand curve is vertical.

=== tf y11-tf-P2f
c: P2f.1 P2f.2 P2f.3 P2f.4
- T: Supply is usually more elastic in the long run than in the short run.
- F: Spare capacity makes supply less elastic. => Spare capacity makes supply more elastic, because firms can raise output quickly.
- T: A perfectly elastic supply curve is horizontal.
- F: Goods that perish quickly have very elastic supply. => Perishable goods that cannot be stored tend to have inelastic supply.

=== tf y11-tf-P2g

- F: An oligopoly is a market with one seller. => A monopoly has one seller; an oligopoly is dominated by a few large firms.
- T: Monopolistic competition has many firms selling differentiated products, with low barriers to entry.
- F: Higher market concentration usually leads to lower prices for consumers. => Higher market concentration usually gives firms more pricing power, so prices tend to rise.
- T: Network effects make a digital platform more valuable to each user as more people use it.

=== tf y11-tf-P2h
c: P2h.1 P2h.2 P2h.3 P2h.5 P2h.6 P2h.7 P2h.8

- F: Public goods are rival and excludable. => Public goods are non-rival and non-excludable.
- T: Merit goods, such as education, tend to be under-consumed when left to the market.
- F: Collusion between firms benefits consumers through lower prices. => Collusion is an abuse of market power that raises prices for consumers.

- T: Land held communally or under native title generally cannot be mortgaged, which can make it harder for some Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander businesses to borrow.

=== tf y11-tf-P2i
