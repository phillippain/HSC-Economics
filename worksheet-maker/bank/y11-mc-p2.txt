# Year 11 (2025 syllabus) multiple choice: Focus area 2, Markets.

=== mc y11-mc-P2a-01
c: P2a.1
lv: 2
Q: When a good becomes scarce and its price rises, the higher price gives firms a reason to produce more of it. This is the price mechanism's
A: signalling function
B: redistribution function
C: rationing function
D: incentive function
ANS: D
WHY: The higher price makes production more profitable, an incentive to supply more. The signalling function is the price change telling people the good has become scarcer; rationing is limiting who buys it.

=== mc y11-mc-P2a-02
c: P2a.1
lv: 2
Q: Concert tickets sell out at a high price, so only people willing and able to pay that price can attend. This shows the price mechanism's
A: signalling function
B: incentive function
C: rationing function
D: stabilisation function
ANS: C
WHY: A higher price rations a scarce good among buyers: those who will not or cannot pay that price go without.

C: owners can be confident they will keep the rewards of their effort and investment

WHY: Secure property rights and enforceable contracts give people the confidence to invest, produce and trade.

=== mc y11-mc-P2b-01
c: P2b.2
lv: 1
Q: The law of demand states that, other things equal,
A: as income rises, demand rises
B: as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded rises
C: as the price of a good rises, the quantity demanded falls
D: as supply rises, demand rises
ANS: C
WHY: The law of demand is the inverse relationship between a good's own price and the quantity demanded, which gives the demand curve its downward slope.

=== mc y11-mc-P2b-02
c: P2b.3 P2b.4
lv: 2
Q: A fall in the price of coffee leads to
A: an increase in demand for coffee: the demand curve shifts right
B: a contraction in the quantity of coffee demanded
C: a decrease in demand for coffee
D: an expansion in the quantity of coffee demanded: a movement along the demand curve
ANS: D
WHY: A change in the good's own price causes a movement along its demand curve. Only a change in a non-price factor shifts the curve.

=== mc y11-mc-P2b-03
c: P2b.4
lv: 2
Q: Which of the following would increase the demand for electric cars?
A: A fall in the price of electric cars
B: A rise in the price of petrol
C: A rise in the price of electricity
D: A fall in consumers' incomes
ANS: B
WHY: Dearer petrol makes petrol cars (substitutes for electric cars) more costly to run, so demand for electric cars increases. A is a movement along the curve; C raises the cost of a complement; D reduces demand for a normal good.

=== mc y11-mc-P2b-04
c: P2b.4
lv: 2
Q: Tea and coffee are substitutes. If the price of tea rises, the demand for coffee will
A: expand along the demand curve for coffee
B: decrease: the demand curve for coffee shifts to the left
C: increase: the demand curve for coffee shifts to the right
D: stay the same
ANS: C
WHY: Some tea drinkers switch to coffee, so more coffee is demanded at every price of coffee.

Q: Effective demand is
A: the desire for a good or service

D: the desire for a good or service backed by the ability and willingness to pay

WHY: Wanting something is not enough; demand only counts in a market when the buyer can and will pay.

=== mc y11-mc-P2c-01

A: at higher prices production is more profitable, so firms supply more
B: consumers buy more at higher prices
C: costs of production fall as prices rise

WHY: A higher price covers the higher costs of extra output and gives firms an incentive to produce more (the law of supply).

=== mc y11-mc-P2c-05
c: P2c.1
lv: 1
Q: The market supply of a good is
A: the quantity one firm will supply at each price
B: the quantity consumers want to buy at each price
C: the sum of the quantities all firms in the market will supply at each price
D: the total stock of the good held in warehouses
ANS: C
WHY: Market supply adds together every firm's individual supply at each price. Supply is effective only when firms are willing and able to produce at that price.

=== mc y11-mc-P2c-04

Q: Cattle farmers expect the price of beef to rise sharply next year, so they hold back cattle from sale now. The supply of beef this year will
A: increase
B: not change
C: expand along the supply curve
D: decrease
ANS: D

=== mc y11-mc-P2d-01
c: P2d.1
lv: 1
Q: Demand for a good increases while supply does not change. The equilibrium price and quantity will
A: price falls and quantity rises
B: price rises and quantity falls
C: both rise
D: both fall
ANS: C
WHY: At the old price there is now a shortage, so the price rises; the higher price causes an expansion of supply, so quantity rises too.

=== mc y11-mc-P2d-02
c: P2d.1 P2d.2
lv: 3
Q: Demand for avocados increases and, at the same time, a bumper harvest increases supply. What happens to the market equilibrium?
A: Price and quantity both rise
B: Price falls and quantity does not change
C: Quantity rises, but the change in price depends on the relative size of the two shifts
D: Price rises, but the change in quantity cannot be known
ANS: C
WHY: Both shifts raise quantity. The demand increase pushes price up and the supply increase pushes it down, so the net effect on price depends on which shift is larger.

=== mc y11-mc-P2d-05
c: P2d.3
lv: 2
Q: A competitive market equilibrium is said to be efficient because
A: firms earn the highest possible profit
B: everyone can afford the good
C: resources are used to produce output up to the point where the value buyers place on the last unit equals the cost of producing it
D: the government sets the price
ANS: C
WHY: At equilibrium, every unit that buyers value at more than its cost is produced, and no unit that costs more than it is worth to buyers is produced (allocative efficiency). It does not mean everyone can afford it.

=== mc y11-mc-P2e-01
c: P2e.3
lv: 2
k: calc
Q: The price of a movie ticket rises by 10 per cent and the quantity demanded falls by 25 per cent. Using the percentage method, the price elasticity of demand is
A: 0.4
B: 0.25
C: 15
D: 2.5
ANS: D
WHY: PED = % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price = −25 ÷ 10 = −2.5, usually written as 2.5. It is greater than 1, so demand is relatively elastic. A divides the wrong way round.

=== mc y11-mc-P2e-02
c: P2e.1
lv: 1
Q: Demand for a good is perfectly inelastic. This means that
A: the quantity demanded does not change when the price changes
B: any price rise causes the quantity demanded to fall to zero
C: the percentage change in quantity equals the percentage change in price
D: the demand curve is horizontal
ANS: A
WHY: Perfectly inelastic demand (PED = 0) is a vertical demand curve. B and D describe perfectly elastic demand; C describes unit elasticity.

=== mc y11-mc-P2e-03

=== mc y11-mc-P2e-06

=== mc y11-mc-P2h-03
c: P2h.5
lv: 2
Q: The free rider problem means that
A: people can benefit from a good without paying for it, so private firms will not supply enough of it
B: the government provides goods free of charge
C: monopolies charge high prices
D: consumers lack information about products
ANS: A
WHY: If people can use a good without paying, few will pay, so a profit-seeking firm will under-provide it. This is why public goods are usually provided by government.

=== mc y11-mc-P2h-04

Q: A used-car seller knows a car has a faulty gearbox, but the buyer does not. This is an example of
A: a positive externality
B: perfect competition
C: a public good
D: asymmetric information
ANS: D
WHY: One side of the transaction knows more than the other, so the buyer may pay more than the car is worth. This can lead to market failure.

=== mc y11-mc-P2h-05

A: predatory pricing
B: consumer sovereignty

ANS: D
WHY: Collusion is an abuse of market power: firms agree not to compete, so consumers pay more. Predatory pricing is setting prices very low to drive rivals out.
