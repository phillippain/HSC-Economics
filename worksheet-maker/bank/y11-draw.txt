Q: To make renting more affordable, the government sets a maximum rent below the equilibrium rent. Draw a diagram to show the effect on the rental market.

=== draw y11-dr-P2i-03

Q: The government increases the excise tax on each packet of cigarettes. Draw a diagram to show the effect on the market for cigarettes.
ANS: The tax raises producers' costs, so supply decreases: the supply curve shifts up by the amount of the tax, from S1 to S2. === draw y11-dr-P2h-01
c: P2h.4 P2h.3
lv: 2
DIA: extern good=electricity from coal
Q: Coal-fired power stations release emissions that harm people living nearby and the climate. Draw a diagram to show this negative externality in production.
ANS: The market supply curve reflects only private costs (MPC). The marginal social cost (MSC) is higher, because of the cost of emissions to others. The market produces Q_m, where D (MPB) = MPC, more than the efficient quantity Qs, where D = MSC. The shaded triangle is the deadweight loss from over-production.

=== draw y11-dr-P2h-02
c: P2h.4 P2h.3
lv: 2
DIA: extern pos good=vaccinations
Q: When a person is vaccinated, others also benefit because the disease spreads less. Draw a diagram to show this positive externality in consumption.
ANS: The demand curve shows private benefits only (D, MPB). The marginal social benefit (MSB) is higher, because of the benefit to others. The market settles at Q_m, where MPB = S, less than the efficient quantity Qs, where MSB = MSC (S). The shaded triangle is the welfare lost through under-consumption; a subsidy or free provision could raise output to Qs.

Q: Education is a merit good. Draw a diagram to show why, left to the market, too little education would be consumed, and explain ONE policy to correct this.

=== draw y11-dr-P3c-01

lv: 2
DIA: lrac
Q: Draw a long-run average cost (LRAC) curve. Label economies of scale, diseconomies of scale and the technical optimum.
ANS: The LRAC curve is U-shaped. While it falls, the firm enjoys economies of scale (e.g. bulk buying, specialisation, better technology). Its lowest point is the technical optimum (output Q*). Beyond it, diseconomies of scale (e.g. coordination and communication problems) push average costs up.
