Policy Simulator

You set the cash rate and the budget in four real episodes, then compare with what was decided

Monetary policy

Set by the Reserve Bank's Monetary Policy Board, independently of the government.

  • Tool: the cash rate target, the interest rate on overnight loans between banks.
  • Goals: inflation of 2–3% on average over time, and full employment.
  • Tightening (contractionary): a higher cash rate lifts borrowing costs and slows spending, to lower inflation.
  • Easing (expansionary): a lower cash rate supports spending and jobs.
  • Time lag: the full effect on inflation can take one to two years.
  • Limit: it works on demand. It cannot fix a supply shock, such as higher oil prices.

Fiscal policy

Set by the Australian Government through the Budget.

  • Tools: government spending and taxation.
  • Expansionary: more spending or lower taxes, moving the budget towards deficit.
  • Contractionary: less spending or higher taxes, moving the budget towards surplus.
  • Automatic stabilisers: in a downturn tax revenue falls and welfare payments rise without any decision.
  • Strength: payments can reach households within weeks and can be targeted.
  • Limits: decisions take time to make and pass, deficits add to public debt, and politics shapes the choices.

How to judge a decision

  • Which goal is most at risk? Inflation, unemployment and growth, or both.
  • Where does the problem come from? Demand that is too strong or too weak, or a supply shock.
  • Do the two policies pull together? The policy mix works best when they do.
  • What will the lag do? Policy set today acts on next year's economy.
  • What is the cost, and who bears it? Borrowers, the unemployed, future taxpayers.

There is rarely one right answer. In November 2026 the Board itself split 6 to 3.

The briefings give the figures decision-makers had at the time; many were later revised. Each episode lists its sources. This is a simplified study tool: real decisions weigh far more information. Check a figure against its source before you quote it in an exam.
