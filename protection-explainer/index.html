Four ideas, one market

Every diagram on this page uses the same market for an imported good. Australia is small, so it takes the world price as given and cannot change it.

Consumer surplus
What buyers would have paid minus what they do pay. On the diagram it is the area under the demand curve and above the price.
Producer surplus
What sellers receive minus the least they would accept. It is the area above the supply curve and below the price they receive.
Deadweight loss
Surplus that no one gets. Trades that would have helped both sides stop happening, and resources go to less efficient uses.
Revenue
Money collected by the government as tariff revenue. It is a transfer, so it is not a loss.
Transfer
Surplus that moves from one group to another, such as consumers to producers. The total is unchanged, so a transfer is not deadweight loss.
Cost to government
What a subsidy costs taxpayers: the subsidy per unit times the units produced. It is outlined in gold on the subsidy diagram.

    Same price, different tools

    A $15 tariff and a 30 million unit quota give the same price, output, consumption and imports. A subsidy leaves consumers alone and sends the bill to taxpayers.

    Where Australia has used these tools

    Each case links back to the diagram. Use these as examples in extended responses, with the figures.

    Tariff, then removal

    The car industry

    Tariffs on motor vehicles were around 60% at the start of the 1980s. The Button plan cut them step by step to 15% by 2000, and they were cut further to 5%. Ford stopped making cars in Australia in 2016, and Holden and Toyota followed in 2017.

    On the diagram: removing a tariff runs the lab backwards. The price falls from P1 to Pw, domestic output falls from Q3 to Q1, and triangles b and d disappear. Consumers gain and local producers lose.

    Tariff, today

    Very low average protection

    The average tariff fell from 36% in 1968–69 to 0.4% in 2025. About 90% of imports are tariff free and the general rate is 5% or less. Tariff revenue is $1.9bn, about 0.2% of government revenue.

    On the diagram: a small t means small triangles and a thin revenue rectangle. Set the tariff to $2 and the deadweight loss is only $4m, because it grows with the square of the tariff.

    Subsidy and export incentives

    Whyalla and the EMDG

    Government support for the Whyalla steelworks is $2.4bn plus $223m. The Export Market Development Grants scheme provides $105m in 2026–27 and has helped more than 51,000 exporters since 1974.

    On the diagram: support that lowers a producer's costs works like a rightward shift of supply from S to S1. Taxpayers fund areas a and b, producers keep a, and b is lost.

    Other countries’ protection

    Wine and farm support abroad

    China’s anti-dumping duties on Australian wine, in place from 2021 to 2024, cut wine exports to China from $1.3bn to under $1m in 2023. They recovered to about $1bn in the year to March 2025. Farm support is under 3% in Australia, against 7% in the US, 16% in the EU and 41% in South Korea.

    On the diagram: read it from the exporter’s side. A foreign tariff lowers the price Australian sellers receive and shrinks their producer surplus. A foreign subsidy lets rivals sell at lower prices.

    Writing it up in the exam

    Markers reward a correct diagram, a clear chain of cause and effect, numbers where you have them, and a judgement when the question asks for one.

    Diagram checklist

      Command words

      • Outline or describe. State what happens to price, output, imports and revenue.
      • Explain. Give the reason for each change, such as why the supply curve is climbed.
      • Analyse. Split the effect by group: consumers, producers, government. Use areas or figures.
      • Assess or evaluate. Weigh the gains (jobs, infant industries, defence, dumping) against the costs (higher prices, deadweight loss, retaliation) and reach a judgement.
      Model answer · Explain the effects of a tariff on an imported good (6 marks)

      A tariff is a tax on imports. It raises the domestic price from the world price (Pw) to Pw + t, labelled P1 on the diagram. At the higher price domestic producers move up their supply curve and raise output from Q1 to Q3, while consumers move up their demand curve and cut purchases from Q2 to Q4. Imports therefore fall from Q2 − Q1 to Q4 − Q3.

      Producers gain surplus (area a) and the government collects revenue equal to the tariff times the new level of imports (area c). Consumers lose surplus equal to a + b + c + d because they pay more and buy less. Triangles b and d are deadweight loss. Resources move into production that the world could supply more cheaply (b), and consumers give up purchases they value above the world price (d). Total welfare falls by b + d, so the tariff protects some firms and jobs at a net cost to the economy.

      Lifting it to the top band

      • Use the figures from the stimulus. Show the working for revenue and for each triangle.
      • Compare tools: a quota gives the same price, output and imports as a tariff of the same size, but it limits the quantity of imports instead of taxing them. A subsidy leaves consumers’ price unchanged and has one triangle.
      • Name a reason for protection from the syllabus (infant industry, domestic employment, dumping, defence) and test it against the cost.
      • Add an Australian example with a figure, then finish with a judgement on who gains, who pays and whether the gain outweighs the loss.

      Mistakes that cost marks

      • Costing tariff revenue on the imports before the tariff.
      • Drawing one deadweight triangle for a tariff or quota. There are two. A subsidy has one.
      • Placing triangle b to the left of the supply curve. It sits to the right of S, between Q1 and Q3.
      • Costing a subsidy on the extra output only. The cost is the subsidy times all domestic output.
      • Drawing a tariff revenue rectangle on a quota diagram. A quota limits the quantity of imports; it is not a tax.
      • Calling a transfer a loss. Areas a and c move between groups. Only b and d are lost.
