Exam question

Band 6 extended response with a scaffold

This was Question 27 in the 2026 HSC (Section IV, 20 marks).

Assess the influence of domestic and global factors on Australia's external stability. (20 marks)

Plan it with the scaffold, write it by hand in 35 minutes, then mark it against the points.

  1. Introduction. Define external stability and name its measures. State your judgement: which group of factors matters more, and for which measure.
  2. Background. The current position in three figures: the deficit, net foreign debt, net foreign liabilities.
  3. Global factor 1: the terms of trade and commodity prices. Cyclical; affects the balance on goods and services and the dollar.
  4. Global factor 2: world interest rates and growth in trading partners. Affects net primary income and export volumes.
  5. Domestic factor 1: the savings–investment gap. Structural; drives net primary income and foreign liabilities. Include superannuation and the budget.
  6. Domestic factor 2: competitiveness and the structure of exports. Narrow export base, productivity, inflation.
  7. Conclusion. Weigh them: domestic factors set the long-run position; global factors explain the swings. Support it with a figure.
Show the marking points
  • Definition: the ability to meet financial obligations to the rest of the world over the medium to long term without sharp swings in the dollar; measured by the current account deficit, net foreign debt and liabilities as % of GDP, the terms of trade, the exchange rate and competitiveness.
  • Current position: deficit $94.9 billion, 3.2% of GDP (2025–26); net foreign debt 50.8% of GDP; net foreign liabilities 21.8% of GDP, down from over 60% in 2015–16.
  • Global: the terms of trade peaked in mid-2022 and fell 18% in three years; the balance on goods and services went from a record surplus (5.2% of GDP in 2021–22) to a deficit of $7.5 billion.
  • Global: higher world interest rates doubled net debt servicing costs to $38 billion in two years; the debt servicing ratio rose from 3.4% to 7.2%; China buys almost a third of exports; US tariffs of 12.5% from July 2026.
  • Domestic: investment exceeds national saving, so liabilities and the net primary income deficit ($85.5 billion) persist; super (12%) has raised saving and made net foreign equity negative; budget deficits widen the gap.
  • Domestic: resources about 57% of exports; competitiveness ranked 17th (IMD 2026); Future Made in Australia and microeconomic reform as responses.
  • The floating exchange rate links the two: it absorbs global shocks, and most debt is in A$.
  • A sustained judgement with current figures, for example: domestic structural factors determine the level of external liabilities, while global cyclical factors determine the year-to-year deficit; external stability is stronger than a decade ago but remains exposed to commodity prices and world interest rates.
  • A relevant diagram (the market for A$, or AD/AS) referred to in the text.
Show a Band 6 opening

External stability is Australia's ability to meet its financial obligations to the rest of the world without sharp swings in the dollar or constraints on growth. Both domestic and global factors shape it, but in different ways: domestic factors, above all the gap between national saving and investment, set the long-run level of foreign liabilities, while global factors such as the terms of trade and world interest rates explain why the deficit moved from a surplus of 2% of GDP to a deficit of 3.2% in four years.
