The current account deficit as a percentage of GDP

The average for each financial year. The dashed line is the International Monetary Fund's guide: a deficit above 4% of GDP in the medium to long term is too high.

Source: Reserve Bank of Australia statistical tables (ABS data). Live: redrawn when new figures are published.

Net foreign debt, net foreign equity and net foreign liabilities

Each as a percentage of GDP. Net foreign liabilities = net foreign debt + net foreign equity. A negative number for equity means Australians own more overseas than foreigners own here.

Source: Reserve Bank of Australia statistical tables (ABS data). Live: redrawn when new figures are published. Levels at the end of each quarter, divided by nominal GDP over the previous four quarters.

The terms of trade

Export prices compared with import prices, as an index. A rise means the same volume of exports buys more imports.

Source: Reserve Bank of Australia statistical tables (ABS data). Live: redrawn when new figures are published.

The terms of trade and the trade balance

Do they move together? The terms of trade index is on the first chart and the balance on goods and services, as a percentage of GDP, is on the second. They are drawn separately because they are measured in different units.

Source: Reserve Bank of Australia statistical tables (ABS data). Live: redrawn when new figures are published.
