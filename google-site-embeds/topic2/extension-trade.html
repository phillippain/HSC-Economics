Risk: almost three-tenths of export income depends on one buyer, so a slowdown in China or a trade dispute (as in 2020, when China restricted wine, barley and coal) cuts export income, the terms of trade and the dollar. Benefit: China's demand for iron ore, coal and gas lifted export income and the terms of trade for two decades, raising national income and funding the mining investment boom.

Band 6 writing: commodity dependence

Assess the risks and benefits for the Australian economy of its dependence on commodity exports. (10 marks)

Plan it with the scaffold, write it by hand in 35 minutes, then mark it against the points.

  1. Define and judge first. Say what commodity dependence means (resources are over half of exports) and give your overall judgement in the first two sentences.
  2. Benefits (two paragraphs). Higher terms of trade and national income; investment, jobs and tax revenue; a trade surplus in boom years.
  3. Risks (two paragraphs). Volatile income; a high dollar in booms that squeezes other exporters; reliance on China; the energy transition.
  4. Evidence in every paragraph. One statistic each, with its year.
  5. Conclude. Weigh the two sides and say what would change your judgement (diversification, critical minerals).
Show the marking points
  • Resources are about 55% of exports (2026), up from about 32% in 1990; iron ore alone was $118.8 billion in 2025–26.
  • Benefit: the terms of trade peaked in mid-2022 at about double their 2003 level, giving a record trade surplus of 5.2% of GDP in 2021–22.
  • Benefit: mining investment, royalties and company tax; four current account surpluses from 2019–20 to 2022–23.
  • Risk: prices fall quickly. The terms of trade fell 18% in the three years after the peak and the balance on goods and services returned to deficit in 2025–26.
  • Risk: concentration. China takes 29.4% of exports; the 2020 trade restrictions showed the exposure.
  • Risk: a commodity currency. The dollar rises in booms, making manufacturing, tourism and education less competitive.
  • Risk: structural change as trading partners cut emissions; thermal coal declines in the long run.
  • Judgement supported by evidence, for example: the benefits have outweighed the risks so far, but diversification (critical minerals, services, new markets such as India) is needed to manage them.
Show a Band 6 opening

Australia depends on commodities for more than half of its export income, and that dependence has made the country richer and more exposed at the same time. On balance the benefits have outweighed the risks over the past two decades, because high export prices lifted national income and paid for record investment; but the fall of 18% in the terms of trade after 2022 shows how quickly that income can be lost.
