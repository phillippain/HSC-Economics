The basis of free trade

Free trade means goods and services move between countries without tariffs, quotas or other barriers. The case for it rests on comparative advantage. A country has a comparative advantage in a good when it can make it at a lower opportunity cost than other countries. If each country specialises in those goods and trades for the rest, total output is higher and every country can consume more.

Advantages of free trade

  • Resources move to the industries that use them best, so output and incomes rise.
  • Consumers get lower prices and more choice.
  • Firms reach larger markets and gain economies of scale.
  • Competition from imports pushes local firms to lift productivity and innovate.

Disadvantages of free trade

  • Structural unemployment rises in industries that cannot compete, often in particular regions.
  • New industries may fail before they are large enough to compete.
  • Production can move to countries with weaker wages, safety or environmental rules.
  • A country can become reliant on imports of essential goods, or on one export market.

Trading blocs, monetary unions and free trade agreements

  • Free trade agreement: members cut tariffs and other barriers on each other's goods and services. A bilateral agreement has two members. A multilateral agreement has many.
  • Trading bloc: a group of countries, usually in one region, with free trade between members. The European Union and ASEAN are examples.
  • Monetary union: members share one currency and one central bank. Twenty-one European Union countries use the euro, and the European Central Bank sets one interest rate for all of them.

Are these agreements good for world trade?

  • For: they open markets faster than global talks, which have stalled at the World Trade Organization. Trade between members grows (trade creation).
  • Against: members may buy from each other when a non-member is cheaper (trade diversion). Blocs can keep high barriers against outsiders, and many overlapping deals add rules and paperwork.
  • Monetary unions: a shared currency removes exchange rate risk and lowers costs. But members cannot set their own interest rate or let their currency fall in a downturn, as Greece found after 2009.

Use it in an exam: Explain comparative advantage in one or two sentences, then weigh an advantage of free trade against a disadvantage. For agreements, name one bloc and one bilateral deal and use the terms trade creation and trade diversion.
