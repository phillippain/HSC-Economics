What they are

A local content rule says that a set share of a good or service must be made in the home country. It is a form of protection. It does not tax imports as a tariff does. It guarantees local producers a share of the market.

How they work

  • A firm must use a minimum share of local parts, workers or programs to sell in the market or to win a government contract.
  • Demand for the local product rises, so local output and jobs are higher than they would be under free trade.
  • The government collects no revenue, unlike a tariff. The cost is hidden in higher prices or less choice.

Australian example: streaming services

A law passed in November 2025 applies from 1 January 2026. Streaming services with more than one million Australian subscribers must invest in new Australian drama, children's, documentary, arts and educational programs. They can spend 10 per cent of what they spend on programs for Australia, or 7.5 per cent of their Australian revenue. The rule is phased in over three years and will be reviewed after four.

Other examples

  • Local content quotas for Australian music on commercial radio.
  • Government purchasing rules that favour local suppliers.
  • Rules of origin in free trade agreements, which set how much of a good must be made in a member country to get the lower tariff.

Use it in an exam: Treat local content rules like a quota. Explain who gains (local producers and workers) and who pays (consumers and firms that face higher costs), then judge the effect on efficiency.
