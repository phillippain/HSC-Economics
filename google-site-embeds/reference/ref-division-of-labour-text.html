What it means

The international division of labour is how the world's jobs are shared between countries. Each stage of production moves to where it can be done at the lowest cost or with the best skills. A phone may be designed in the United States, use parts made in South Korea and be put together in China or India.

Two ways labour is used globally

  • People move to the jobs (migration): skilled workers move to high-wage countries. Low-skilled workers fill shortages in jobs such as farm work, building and aged care.
  • Jobs move to the people (offshoring): firms shift factories and services such as call centres to countries with lower labour costs.

Effects

  • Receiving countries fill skill shortages and slow the ageing of their workforce. Australia relies on skilled migration for this.
  • Sending countries gain remittances, which is money workers send home. They can also lose skilled workers. This is called the brain drain.
  • Workers: labour moves far less freely than money or goods because of visa rules, language and the cost of moving. Wages still differ widely between countries.

Use it in an exam: Contrast labour with finance: capital moves in seconds, but only about 1 in 27 people live outside their country of birth. Use remittances to show a benefit for developing economies.

Sources: UN DESA, International Migrant Stock 2024; ILO, Global Estimates on International Migrant Workers; World Bank, Migration and Development Brief.
