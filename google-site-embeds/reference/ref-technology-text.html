Why it matters

Technology is a main driver of globalisation. It cuts the cost and time of moving goods, services, money and information between countries, so more things can be traded.

How each one drives globalisation

  • Transport: container ships, bulk carriers and air freight lower the cost per unit of moving goods. Australia can sell iron ore to Asia and buy cars from Japan at a low freight cost.
  • Communication: the internet, mobile phones and video calls let firms run global supply chains and sell services such as education, finance and software across borders.
  • Finance: electronic payments and trading move money between countries in seconds. This has grown financial flows faster than trade in goods.
  • Production: automation and artificial intelligence raise productivity, but they also change which jobs are needed and where.

The digital divide

Access is not equal. Almost everyone in high-income countries is online, but fewer than one in four people in low-income countries are. Countries without access miss out on the gains from trade in services. This widens the gap in living standards between nations.

Use it in an exam: Link technology to lower costs, then to higher trade and financial flows. Use the digital divide as evidence that the gains from globalisation are shared unequally.
