Government economic forums

  • G20: leaders of the largest advanced and emerging economies. Australia is a member. It coordinates responses to global problems, as it did in the 2008 global financial crisis.
  • G7: the United States, Japan, Germany, the United Kingdom, France, Italy and Canada. Australia is not a member but is often invited.

How well do they work?

  • Strengths: shared rules lower trade barriers, crisis loans stop problems spreading, and forums let countries act together.
  • Limits: decisions need agreement, so progress is slow. The WTO's Doha Round of talks stalled, and its appeals body has not been able to hear cases since 2019. Large economies can act alone, as the United States has with tariffs.
  • Criticism: IMF loan conditions can force spending cuts that hurt the poor. Voting power in the IMF and World Bank favours rich countries.

Use it in an exam: Name the body, state its role, give one example of it acting, then judge it. A top answer weighs a strength against a limit.

Sources: WTO; IMF; World Bank; OECD; G20.
