What they do

A free trade agreement (FTA) removes or lowers tariffs and other barriers between the countries that sign it. A bilateral agreement has two members. A multilateral or regional agreement has many.

Why Australia signs them

  • World Trade Organization talks have stalled, so deals with single partners or regions are the faster way to open markets.
  • Exporters of beef, wine, minerals and services gain lower tariffs in large markets.
  • Consumers and firms pay less for imports.
  • Deals with many partners spread risk. The government has pushed to diversify trade away from reliance on China.

The newest deals

  • United Arab Emirates: in force from 1 October 2025. It is Australia's first agreement in the Middle East.
  • European Union: talks concluded on 24 March 2026 after years of dispute over farm access. It starts once both sides have signed and ratified it.

Limits

  • Trade diversion: buying from a partner because its goods are tariff free, even when another country makes them more cheaply.
  • Rules of origin add paperwork, so some firms do not use the lower rates.
  • Sensitive sectors, such as farm goods, are often left out or opened slowly.

Use it in an exam: Name two agreements with dates, explain trade creation and trade diversion, and judge whether bilateral deals are a good substitute for global free trade.
