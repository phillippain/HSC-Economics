Updated 14 November 2026

Value

Trade is worth close to half of Australia's GDP when exports and imports are added. Export values swing with commodity prices. When iron ore and coal prices fall, export income falls even if volumes hold.

Composition: what Australia trades

  • Exports are led by minerals and energy. Iron ore, coal and natural gas are the three largest. Services such as education and tourism come next, then farm goods.
  • Imports are mostly manufactured goods and fuel: refined petroleum, cars, phones and computers. Refined petroleum imports rose 25.5 per cent to $55.1 billion in 2025-26 as fuel prices climbed.
  • This pattern reflects comparative advantage. Australia is rich in resources and land, and has a small manufacturing base.

Direction: who Australia trades with

Trade has shifted from Europe to Asia. China is the largest partner by a wide margin, then Japan, South Korea, the United States and India. Relying on one buyer is a risk, as China's trade restrictions in 2020 showed.

Financial flows

  • Australia saves less than it invests, so it borrows from and sells assets to the rest of the world. This is a financial account surplus that matches the current account deficit.
  • The United States and the United Kingdom are the largest sources of foreign investment. Most is portfolio investment (shares and debt), not direct investment.
  • Australia owes more than it owns overseas, so net income flows out each quarter.

Use it in an exam: Give one figure for each of value, composition and direction, then explain a trend. Link the export mix to the terms of trade and the current account.
