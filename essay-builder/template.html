The economics paragraph

The paragraph structure from the course booklet. Use it for every body paragraph in Sections III and IV.

Repeat E → D → E. Two or three cycles a paragraph; each must add a new mechanism or new data.

Use the stimulus. In Section III, cite the stimulus by its source in at least one cycle of every paragraph.

A diagram earns marks only when it is accurate, labelled and explained in the paragraph around it. Refer to it in the text before you draw it.

Plan each diagram. In step 3, choose the model, choose what changes (the sketch redraws), name the cause from your Explain chain, write the caption that says what it shows and note where you refer to it in the text. Figures are numbered across the whole response, and step 4 lists them all.

What the directive asks for

The key words in a question tell you what kind of answer earns the marks. Many questions have more than one.

Short answers: Section II

Marks and lines show how much depth is needed. Use the stimulus when there is one, and show all working in a calculation.

Outline

General, big-picture information. An opening statement, then one clear point for each mark.

Explain

Show how or why, linking cause and effect. An opening statement or definition, then for each point: a description, the effect (why or how) and an example. Finish with one or two sentences linking back to the question.

Words that show cause and effect

  • because
  • since
  • therefore
  • consequently
  • as a result
  • for this reason
  • subsequently
  • this means
  • leads to
  • results in

The whole response: Sections III and IV

Plan first. About 35 minutes for each response, including the plan. Aim for about four pages.

Introduction. Define the key terms from the question and state your point of view. Do not just restate the question.

Body. One major idea in each paragraph, fully explained, developed and supported. Go back to the wording of the question regularly.

Conclusion. Draw the strands of your argument together. Nothing at odds with the body, and no new evidence.

Data. A top-band response relates the theory to what is happening now in the Australian and global economy, with accurate, dated statistics.

Choose your level of support

The support switch sits above step 2. Each level adds to the one before it.

On my own. Prompts and examples only. This is how you will plan in the exam.

Sentence starters. Starters and linking words under each box. Click one, then finish the sentence.

Guiding questions. Questions for each part of a paragraph, and five steps that build a draft thesis from your answers.

Suggested ideas. Possible points, cause and effect chains from the Chain Builder and evidence that fits. Reword them to answer the question.

Worked model. A model thesis and paragraph for a similar question, with each part of the paragraph marked.

Step down. Start where you need to, then drop a level each time you plan a response. Aim to plan on your own before the exam.

Your own evidence

You are not limited to the Evidence Finder. In step 3, open My evidence in the evidence list.

Statistic. A figure from your own reading: say what it measures, its source and its date.

Case study. Facts, strategies and indicators for your case study economy.

Stimulus. A figure or quote from the question's stimulus. It stays with that question.

Example. A policy, event or quote.

Kept on this device. Your evidence is offered in every plan, but only in this browser. Save a backup file from My evidence, and load it on another device.

Diagrams you can use

The builder suggests diagrams that fit the question. Draw large, title it, label both axes and every curve, and mark the before and after points. Curve labels follow the booklet: use AD₁ → AD₂ when both curves are named, or AD → AD₁ if your teacher asks for the booklet's page 43 style. Use AS for the upward-sloping aggregate supply curve; call it SRAS only when LRAS is on the diagram too.
